Layout cookbook

Each slide states one rule and demonstrates it. Read it beside examples/03-layout.md.

This deck is checked by scripts/check-layout.mjs in CI, so every rule here is verified, not asserted.

Rule 1

Bands get the share you draw

+--------+------------+
|  side  |  main      |
+--------+------------+

side : main = 8 : 12

Row heights work the same way: a band of five lines gets five shares.

The drawing is the specification, not a sketch.

Borders are not part of a band, so a one-line heading band is genuinely one line tall — even though the ASCII makes it look bigger.

Draw the band as tall as the content deserves.

Rule 2

A heading needs two lines, three if it wraps

Too tight

+-------------+
|  head       |
+-------------+

An eyebrow plus a heading does not fit one share. The heading is clipped, and a pane with a background below it looks like it ate the title.

Right

+-------------+
|             |
|  head       |
+-------------+

One blank line is usually enough. The checker tells you when it is not.

Rule 3

Alignment is per pane

Default

Content starts at the top. Best when a pane is nearly full.

valign=middle

Centred in the band. Looks deliberate for short content, airy for tall content.

Empty cells

A blank cell in the drawing reserves space. It is the cleanest way to add breathing room.

Rule 4

Anchor near the edge that faces the figure

Between two boxes a connector is the right tool. From a sentence it usually is not — see Rule 5.

When you do use one, give it a clear path: it leaves through the top or bottom of the underline, never sideways. Put the anchored phrase at the end of a line, or on one of its own.

the request first hits the cache

on a miss it reaches the origin

Rule 5

Point at the data, not at a picture

Every mark has an id: #cook-<series>-<row>.

Origin traffic keeps falling — by Q3 it is the smaller half

Press →: the phrase and the bar light up together, in one colour.

Rule 6

Over a photo, buy contrast before you spend it

Bare photo

Every word competes with the picture behind it.

dim=0.5 blur=3

The photo becomes texture, and the words come forward.

Same image, same text, both panes. Only the treatment differs.

Three edges, not four

Rule 7

A photograph can take one half

.bleed runs a background past the grid's margin, on the edges that pane reaches. This one is in the left column, so it goes out left, top and bottom, and stops where these words begin.

The margin on this side is the one the deck sets, untouched: bleeding is a statement about one pane, not about the slide.

The photo is drawn as a column like any other. Only .bleed differs, and it costs the pane beside it nothing.

Rule 9

Let the checker find what your eye misses

node scripts/check-layout.mjs --build examples/03-layout.md

It renders every slide and reports what HTML snapshots cannot see:

  • clipped — content taller or wider than its pane
  • overlap — an overflowing pane running into its neighbour
  • connector — declared but not drawn, usually a typo in an id

This deck passes it in CI, which is the only reason to trust the rules above.

Rule 10

Draw a shape once, not once per slide

masters: masters/cookbook.md

## contrast

+--------------+-------------+
|  head                      |
+--------------+-------------+
|  bad         |  good       |
+--------------+-------------+

<!-- layout: contrast -->

Rules 2 and 6 draw no grid at all: both name contrast, which lives in examples/masters/cookbook.md. A heading names a shape and the pane block under it is the drawing.

A slide's own block always wins, so an exception needs no opt-out — and <!-- layout: none --> gives a title slide the whole surface back.

The catch is names: the master fixes them, so bad and good are what every slide using it calls its panes.

The deck renders identically to the version that drew all three grids by hand. That is the test: a master is a shape moved, not a shape changed.

Rule 11

Balance short items across columns

A list of one-word items runs down the pane's left edge and leaves the rest of the width empty. columns=2 on the pane balances its content by amount: ten items become five and five, and adding one redistributes the rest.

An item never breaks across the fold, and the gutter is the grid gap unless --mz-columns-gap says otherwise.

  • Latency
  • Throughput
  • Cache hits
  • Cold starts
  • Error rate
  • Saturation
  • Queue depth
  • Retries
  • Backoff
  • Timeouts

The split is the browser's, not the author's: the same source with three items fewer still comes out even. For two different things side by side, draw two panes.
